Roof fit

What the six-panel row needs, and what to do if it doesn’t fit

Length the row needs233 in

Six panels at 30.3 in, 1 in gaps, 10.5 in for the combiner before the AC, 29 in of AC, and 7.5 in after it. In the model it runs from x 12 to x 245.

Roof the model used~300 in, estimate

The model treats the roof as flat from the rear cap over the cab-over. The row starts 12 in from the rear cap and ends 11 in past the house-to-cab-over seam (x 234). The 1 in margin quoted earlier compared the row with the house section alone. Measure both sections.

MaxxFan under a panelNo

A MaxxFan Deluxe with rain dome stands 5.0 in tall closed and 9.1 in open. Panels on 2.5 in brackets can’t pass over it.

Fallbacks, in order

  1. Tall rails over the fans. Raise the panels so their underside sits about 10 in off the roof, clearing an open MaxxFan. Panel tops end up about 11.4 in high, still under the Chill Cube’s 14.5 in, so the coach’s overall height doesn’t change. The cost is a heavier rack and more wind load.
  2. Leave a fan open. Shift the row so a fan falls in a gap. Each uncovered fan costs one panel slot, leaving five panels: 1,375 W wired 3 + 2. Two panels in parallel need no per-panel fuses.
  3. Short roof. Drop the front panel: five panels need about 202 in. Four panels (2 + 2, 1,100 W) need about 171 in.
  4. Tighten the gaps. Move the combiner off the row to the roof edge to save about 6 in.

Rear axle check

Axle loads added by the build, against the Transit 350 ratings

The coach’s empty axle weights aren’t published, so the table works backwards. It totals everything the build and a loaded trip add to each axle, then shows the heaviest the empty axle can weigh and still stay under its rating (front 4,630 lb, rear 7,275 lb). Included: the build at its modelled positions, a full fresh tank (290.5 lb at the tank), a 40 lb tow bar on the receiver, a 200 lb driver, and 350 lb of gear spread midship.

  • What it means. With an empty coach around 9,000 lb, the rear axle stays legal if the empty rear axle weighs no more than about 5,790–6,140 lb, or roughly 64–68% of the empty weight on the rear. The front axle has room in every case: it can carry up to 4,150–4,370 lb empty. A CAT scale reading of the empty coach answers it.
  • Gear placement matters as much as the box. Putting the 350 lb of gear in the rear garage instead of midship adds another 177 lb to the rear axle. Every 100 lb moved from the garage to midship takes about 50 lb off the rear.
  • Why the lounge. With the packs and RV5 in the lounge base, about 68–92 in ahead of the rear axle, the build puts about 213 lb less on the rear axle than with the packs in a tail box, and nothing hangs under the coach.

Battery cable

The 48 V run from the packs to the RV5 is under 1 ft

Both sit in the same lounge seat base, with an 8 in gap between the battery stack and the RV5. The pack fuse goes at the pack terminals. The alternator line from the Orions under the passenger seat is about 6 ft at about 16 A, and the PV trunks from the roof gland directly above are about 12 ft each.

Fit checks

Nine things the tape measure has to confirm

  1. Lounge seat base. Measure the inside of the driver-side lounge base: length (needs about 52 in for the stack, the 8 in gap and the RV5), depth from the wall (needs about 20 in for the RV5 plus the front-panel gear), and clear height (needs about 12.5 in for the stacked packs). Check what the factory put in there: furnace, water heater, tank or wiring.
  2. RV5 airflow. The manual asks for 7.87 in of open air at the RV5. Inside the seat base it gets 8 in on the side facing the packs and a louvred front panel; the end wall and front gap are closer. Confirm which faces vent, and add a small fan if needed.
  3. Roof fans and skylight under the panels. The panels sit 2.5 in off the roof, and the model puts two roof fans and the bath skylight under them. A MaxxFan Deluxe with rain dome is 23.2 × 16.6 in and stands 5.0 in tall closed and 9.1 in with the lid open (retailer spec, overall unit height). It cannot sit under a panel on 2.5 in brackets. See the roof fallback section.
  4. Roof length and AC position. The row of six spans 233 in from the first panel edge to the last (x 12 to 245). The model treats the roof as flat from the rear cap over the cab-over to about x 300. Measure the flat roof length, whether the cab-over roof is flat and walkable, and the AC opening’s distance from the rear cap.
  5. Passenger seat swivel. The cab seats swivel to face the table. Check what’s under the passenger swivel base and whether two Orions (each about 7.3 × 5.1 × 3.1 in) fit without blocking the swivel. Fallback: the floor beside the seat base.
  6. Generator bay, shore inlet and breaker panel. Find the generator bay, the shore inlet and the factory breaker panel. The 120 V feeds come in under the floor to the lounge base; their lengths depend on where those are.
  7. Washer corner. Measure the lift bed’s underside fully lowered at the forward curbside corner (needs at least 33.7 in), the floor space from the pantry wall back (needs 23½ in) and out from the curbside wall (needs 22⅝ in), where the bath and galley supply lines run, and where the grey tank inlet is.
  8. Cargo doors. The factory floorplan shows large cargo doors in the rear wall and on the curbside under the bed. The curbside door swings outward, so it still opens; the washer takes about 4½ in off the forward end of its roughly 33 in opening. Measure the opening.
