### The goal of the build

- **Who and how:** One person living and working in it **full-time**.
- **Work:** It's a **mobile office for remote creative and video-editing work**, with a seated desk, monitors, and Starlink satellite internet.
- **Off-grid:** It needs to **boondock** well, meaning camp without hookups and run on battery, solar, and the alternator, with the generator as backup. Hot-climate use (for example Las Vegas summers, around 100°F) has come up repeatedly as a design case.
- **Laundry:** A washer-dryer on board is a hard requirement from the owner.
- **Towing:** The owner will **flat-tow** a **manual-transmission Fiat 500 Abarth** (all four wheels on the ground). Only the manual version can be flat-towed. That means the rear hitch receiver is reserved for the tow bar.
- **Owner's rules for the plan:** Keep his design as drawn. Don't cut the washer or other features to save money or space. Quote prices only from real listings. Buy nothing without his explicit go-ahead. **Earn first, tape-measure a real unit before any deposit.**

## 2. Plain-language glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Boondocking** | Camping with no power, water, or sewer hookups |
| **House battery / bank** | The batteries that run the living area, separate from the van's starter battery |
| **48 V system** | A battery bank at about 48–51 volts. Higher voltage means less current for the same power, so thinner wires and less heat than 12 V |
| **kWh / Wh** | Energy stored or used. 1 kWh = 1,000 watts for one hour |
| **Inverter** | Turns battery DC power into 120 V household AC power. It loses some energy doing it |
| **PV input / MPPT** | A solar input on a charge controller. MPPT is the kind of controller that pulls the most power from the panels |
| **Voc / Isc** | A panel's open-circuit voltage (highest voltage, no load) and short-circuit current (highest current). Used to check controller limits and to size fuses |
| **Vmp / Imp** | A panel's voltage and current at its normal working point |
| **STC** | Standard Test Conditions (77°F cell temperature). Panel voltage rises in cold weather |
| **Series / parallel** | Series wiring adds voltages. Parallel wiring adds currents |
| **Max series fuse rating** | The largest fuse the panel maker allows in line with one panel. It protects the panel if current flows backward into it |
| **Back-feed / reverse current** | When one panel in a parallel group faults, the other panels can push current backward into it |
| **Blocking diode** | A one-way electrical valve that stops back-feed |
| **Combiner** | A box that joins several panels' wires, usually with a fuse for each panel |
| **DC-DC charger** | Charges the house bank from the van's alternator while driving. Here it's 12 V in and 48 V out |
| **SOC** | State of charge, meaning how full the battery is |
| **BTU** | A heating/cooling capacity unit. 15,000 BTU is a typical RV rooftop AC |
| **Ducted vs non-ducted AC** | Ducted units push air through ceiling ducts to vents around the coach. Non-ducted units blow straight down from one box under the unit |
| **Heat pump** | An AC that can run in reverse to heat |
| **Soft start** | A device that cuts the big current spike when an AC compressor starts, so a battery inverter or small generator can start it |
| **Variable-speed (inverter) compressor** | An AC compressor that slows down once the room is cool, using far less power. A soft start alone doesn't make a unit variable-speed |
| **Grey / black tank** | Grey holds sink, shower, and washer water. Black holds toilet waste |
| **IP65 / IP68** | Dust and water resistance ratings. IP65 means protected against water jets. It doesn't mean submersible |
| **AWG** | Wire size. Smaller numbers are thicker wire (2 AWG is thicker than 4 AWG) |
| **GVWR / GAWR / GCWR / CCC / UVW** | Weight limits. See Section 1 |
| **Yellow sticker** | The weight label inside each coach, giving the as-built empty weight and cargo capacity |
| **Lever arm** | Weight hung behind the rear axle loads the rear axle by *more* than its own weight and lightens the front |

**Why parallel, not series:** Two panels in series reach 2 × 25.26 = **50.52 V** open-circuit at 77°F, already over the RV5's 50 V input limit, and voltage rises in cold weather. Series is out on this hub.

**Why 3 + 3:** All six on one input would be 6 × 13.69 = **82.14 A** short-circuit, over the 50 A limit. Three per input is **41.07 A** short-circuit and about 825 W per input. That's under 50 A, but **if a 125% safety factor applies on top, it's 51.3 A, which is over.** Whether Bluetti's 50 A rating already includes that margin is unanswered.

**Roof fit:** The AC sits in the middle of the roof's width. On a roof about 84 in wide, that leaves roughly 27 in on each side, which is narrower than a 30.16 in panel. So **no panel fits beside the AC**, and the AC takes up the full roof width wherever it sits. There are two ways to lay the panels out:
- **2 rows of 3, long side running front to back** (concept sheet D-06): about 205 in of length for the panels plus the AC band, so roughly 245 in or more in total.
- **One row of 6, long side running across the coach** (the 3D model): 6 × 30.3 in plus gaps, about 187 in, split around the AC. That's about **233 in** in total and needs about 68.4 in of clear width.

The house's flat roof is modelled at about **234 in**, which is an estimate. **Roof length and the AC's position along it are the first tape item.** If the roof is short, the array drops to five panels. [ESTIMATE; tape item]

**Bifacial note:** These panels can collect light from the back. On a flat RV roof that's mostly irrelevant. They were chosen for size and electrical fit to the RV5's 12–50 V window.

### 3.5 The 3-panel fuse and diode verdict

This is the main open electrical-safety question.

- **Confirmed:** The panel's **maximum series fuse is 25 A** (Callsun product page and manual).
- **Unstated:** Callsun's public documents **neither allow nor forbid three panels in parallel.** The manual covers parallel wiring with Y-branch connectors, says to use identical panels, and says to check the controller's limits. It gives no maximum parallel count.
- **The concern:** With three panels in parallel, if one panel shorts, the other two can push about **2 × 13.69 A = 27.4 A** backward into it. That's **more than its 25 A maximum fuse rating.**
- **Built-in protection:** The manual says the panel has "built-in reverse-current protection" so it can be left connected to a battery overnight. That's about battery back-flow at night. It isn't guidance on panel-to-panel back-feed in a parallel group.
- **Current plan:** Put **one fuse per panel, never larger than 25 A.** The engineering sheet notes 1.56 × Isc = 21.4 A, which puts a 25 A fuse in range. **Don't upsize** the panel fuse to "cover" back-feed. Each input gets its own combiner box with per-panel fusing, plus a PV breaker or fuse ahead of each RV5 input, with the rating to come from Bluetti.
- **Options before buying:** (a) get **Callsun's written OK** for three in parallel, (b) add **blocking diodes** or listed per-panel fuse holders, or (c) **restructure** the array (for example 2 + 1, or a different split across inputs).
- **Status:** A written question to Callsun is drafted and **held for the owner's approval** (Appendix A). No fuse size has been invented.
- **Team position (please challenge):** A fuse on each panel is the standard fix for exactly this case. If one panel faults, the other two feed it about 27 A, more in strong sun, and that panel's ≤25 A fuse is there to blow. US code (NEC 690.9) requires per-module protection once parallel fault current can exceed the module's series fuse rating, which is the case here. Blocking diodes aren't needed for this; each would waste roughly 6 W of heat at 13 A. So the Callsun answer is worth having, but the team doesn't think it should block the build. [CREDIBLE, general PV practice; not Callsun-specific]
- **On the 125% factor:** That multiplier is for sizing wire and fuses. It isn't normally applied to a charge controller's input rating. The real question for Bluetti is whether the 50 A is a limit on short-circuit current or on operating current. [CREDIBLE; confirm with Bluetti]

### 3.8 Battery placement: decided, please check it

**Reviewer:** Please check whether this is sound. In particular, look at water, heat, service access, and the rear-axle load below.

**Background: the October 6 engineering study** (draft pull request #1, see Section 6). It was written to a brief that wanted the bank **out of the cabin**, and it ranks underbody options:
   1. **A sealed, bolted tail enclosure** behind the rear axle for the two packs. It's first only if the tape shows space and the rear axle still has margin after the lever-arm math.
   2. **Split the packs,** one aft and one near the axle.
   3. **Both packs between the frame rails, on the rear axle.**

   The study **ruled out:** bare packs beside the spare tire (road spray), a hitch-mounted box (the hitch belongs to the Fiat tow bar), and roof-mounted batteries. It also says that if the RV5 can't get its 7.87 in of air inside a sealed box, the hub moves to a dry exterior bay and only the packs go underneath.

- **For it:** the cabin and garage stay free, and the bank sits low, under the floor.
- **Against it:** more rear-axle load than inside, road spray and water exposure (the packs are IP65 but the manual says not to use them if wet), winter cold (self-heating watts are unpublished), and harder service access.
- **Design answers:** a sealed box with a baffled vent and drain, a skid plate, and a lockable door. The hub stays inside, and the main disconnect is reachable without crawling under the coach.

## 5. Build order and dependencies

This sequence comes from the gates and dependencies in the planning documents. It's a logical order, not an approved schedule, and there's no purchase date.

**Phase 0: Before buying the coach (current phase)**
1. **Send the Bluetti question now.** It costs nothing, and every power decision waits on it:
   - Can third-party chargers (the Orions) connect directly to the B4810 packs?
   - Does the RV5 still show an accurate battery % with charging it can't see?
   - What PV fuse or breaker is needed, and which cable gauge?
   - Are the packs approved in a sealed underbody box with the hub inside, and what's the maximum Ethernet length between the hub and the packs?
   - *Dependency:* The Bluetti answer decides the whole power architecture (Section 7). Nothing in the Bluetti or solar stack gets bought until it's in writing.
   - The **Callsun** question (three panels in parallel) is worth sending too, but it's lower priority. Per-panel fuses are the default either way (Section 3.5).
2. **Earn and fund first.** Financing is parked. No deposit.
3. **Tape-measure a real unit** with the field sheet. In priority order:
   - Roof length and the AC opening's position.
   - Tail space, frame-extension height, spare, and receiver positions.
   - The yellow sticker (empty weight, cargo capacity).
   - Drawer-stack width, and the garage depth and height under the lift bed.
   - Grey tank, model year, alternator rating on the unit, shore inlet amps, and bay doors.
   - *Dependency:* This settles the battery box fit, the washer location, and the array size.
4. **Weigh each axle** on a CAT scale. Then decide go or no-go on that unit, then the deposit and purchase. After purchase, weigh the coach plus the Fiat against GCWR.

**Phase 1: Power core (after purchase)**
5. Fabricate and bolt on the **sealed underbody box** for both B4810s. Mount the **RV5 inside the rear garage** with 7.87 in of air around it. Run short, fused 48 V cables of the confirmed gauge, fit a reachable main disconnect, and put the Epad at the desk or entry.
6. Connect the **12 V coach loads** (lights, pump, fans, fridge, Starlink Mini) to the RV5's DC output. Connect **shore and generator** through a transfer switch and a **surge protector / EMS** to its AC input.

**Phase 2: Charging sources**
7. **Roof:** rack, six panels (3 + 3), two combiners with per-panel ≤25 A fuses, PV breakers, and roof penetrations sealed with written roof-warranty coverage from the installer. *Depends on:* panel stock (currently sold out) and roof measurements.
8. **Two Orion chargers** for alternator charging, ignition-switched, with input fuses. *Depends on:* the Bluetti answer.

**Phase 3: AC**
9. Remove the factory unit. Install the **Chill Cube 18K ducted heat pump** in the 14×14 opening with its **ducted air distribution box**, and the optional wall thermostat. Then test cooling on the battery alone through the RV5 and log the watts. *Depends on:* Furrion confirming the air distribution box mates to the factory ducts.

**Phase 4: Living and working**
10. **Washer (WDV2200XCD, vented)** at location A or B: cabinet, 15 A dedicated circuit, hot and cold water, standpipe, sidewall dryer vent, mounting bracket. *Depends on:* the tape (stack width or garage space), the grey-tank size, and the floor requirement from the WDV2200XCD manual.
11. **Rear office desk** (29–30 in, open kneehole) and monitor power on a separate branch from the washer.
12. **Starlink Mini**, cable routing.
13. **Tow setup** for the Fiat (base plate, tow bar, supplemental brake, lighting), then a combined weigh.

14. Finish board (interior materials).

## 7. Open risks and unresolved decisions

### A. Electrical (biggest technical risks)

1. **The whole power architecture depends on one Bluetti answer.** The Orions charge the packs directly, bypassing the RV5. Bluetti's public manuals **don't say** whether this is allowed, or whether the RV5's battery % stays accurate with charging it can't see. (The B4810 manual does permit "an AC-DC charger, DC-DC charger, or other compatible devices," and says its voltage-to-SOC table is only valid at rest. If each pack's own BMS counts current in and out, the % may stay accurate. That's CREDIBLE, not confirmed.)
   - **If yes:** build as planned.
   - **If no, Option A:** keep the RV5, drop to **three panels on PV1 (about 825 W)**, and use the RV5's built-in **600 W alternator charging on PV2**. No Orions. **The team leans to A** because it keeps the parallel wiring and the Bluetti warranty.
   - **If no, Option B:** an **open 48 V system** (three LiTime 150 Ah batteries, a Victron inverter, a Victron MPPT 250/70). This **forces the panels into series**, because a Victron MPPT needs panel voltage above the 48 V battery (about 60 V to start) and parallel panels only reach about 21 V. **No setup gets both parallel panels and the open system.**
2. **3-in-parallel back-feed (~27.4 A) exceeds the 25 A panel fuse rating.** Callsun's documents are silent on it. Per-panel ≤25 A fuses are the planned answer (Section 3.5).
3. **Three panels per input is close to the limit.** 41.07 A vs a 50 A limit. The 125% factor (51.3 A) is a wiring-sizing rule, so ask Bluetti what the 50 A covers.
4. **Alternator capacity.** 250 A is CREDIBLE from a spec listing. Read it on the actual unit.
5. **The battery-cable fuse contradiction.** The RV5 manual's wiring table (garbled in text extraction) includes both "no fuse needed with the B4810" and a "~200 A" fuse note. The cable from the hub inside to the packs underneath passes through the floor, so **fuse it at the packs** regardless, and get the rating in writing.
6. **The RV5 needs air and has no published water rating.** That's why it stays inside and only the packs go underneath.
7. **Energy budget is an estimate** (Section 3.15). The biggest unknowns are the Chill Cube's running watts and the RV5's idle draw.
8. **No surge protection** on the shore inlet yet. A hardwired surge protector / EMS is proposed.

### B. Air conditioning

9. **Chill Cube running and battery-saver watts aren't published.** That's the largest hole in the energy budget.
10. **Duct fit.** The ducted air distribution box has to mate to the factory ceiling ducts. Confirm with Furrion before buying.
11. **Size conflict.** The retailer gives two sizes (29.5 × 29 × 14.5 in vs 27.5 × 27.5 × 11.5 in). Check roof clearance to the panels and the 11 ft 5 in overall height.
12. **Shared inverter limit.** AC, washer (11 A, about 1,300 W), and induction cooktop all share 5,000 W.
13. **Hot-climate performance** at around 100–110°F outside isn't independently published.

### C. Weight and towing

14. **Cargo capacity is unpublished.** One sticker reportedly read 1,765 lb [TEAM NOTE]. One listing says about 1,969 lb [CREDIBLE]. The modelled build is **about 699 lb (or about 914 lb with the heavy panel figure)**, before about 290 lb of fresh water, tow gear, and personal gear. At 1,765 lb, roughly 350–450 lb of margin is left after water and 300–400 lb of gear, at the light panel figure [ESTIMATE].
15. **Panel weight units** (29.8 lb or 29.8 kg each) swing the total by about 215 lb, all of it on the roof.
16. **GCWR headroom is only 2,500 lb** with the coach at GVWR, and the Fiat weighs 2,491–2,512 lb. This is only a problem if the coach is actually at GVWR. **Weigh both together.**
17. **Rear-axle load.** The modelled whole build adds **+704 lb (washer at A) or +796 lb (washer at B)** to the rear axle against a 7,275 lb rating. The coach's actual axle weights are unknown.

### D. Layout and liveability

18. **Washer location, A or B.** A needs a drawer stack ≥24 in wide. B takes the garage and loads the rear axle more.
19. **Grey tank** (30 gal on the 22NF) is unconfirmed. A 16 gal heavy wash is half of it.
20. **Desk plus washer plus garage storage** may not all fit at the rear, especially with location B and the RV5 hub in the garage.
21. **Interior width and ceiling height** aren't published (81 in vs 7 ft conflict), so desk headroom is unknown.

### E. Supply and vendor items

22. **Callsun 275 W is sold out** at $249.99. A stock watch is needed. No equivalent panel with the same dimensions was found in stock from Callsun. Amazon stock wasn't verified.
23. **Bluetti kit pricing was unavailable** on Bluetti's site (October 6).
24. **Held vendor emails** (both waiting on the owner's go-ahead, neither sent): the **Bluetti** technical question and the **Callsun** 3-parallel question. See Appendix A.

### F. Coach-level

25. **Model-year ambiguity.** Coach vs chassis year, 2026 vs 2027, and AWD vs 2WD on one listing. The soft-start question no longer applies.
26. **Roof-penetration warranty:** Get the installer's written coverage of roof penetrations and the AC swap so the coach warranty isn't voided. [TEAM NOTE]
27. **Frame attachment:** The battery box bolts to the coach builder's frame extension behind Ford's frame. Bolted, not welded, and outside Ford's no-drill zones. Confirm the extension's strength.
